# MLB The Show 26 (`0100AA7024094000`)

<!-- PEANUTS: a Peanuts Native profile and note (Banana Native has none for this title). -->

Update 1.0.19 (v1245184) on base v65536. SDK 19.3.11. San Diego Studio's own engine with NVN graphics. Modules `main` and `sdk` only: no `subsdk`, and `main` imports `nn::ro` but the RomFS holds no `.nro` or `.nrr` files.

**Status (current): plays on an AYN Thor and a Sony Xperia 1 V (Turnip Gen8 V39) and a Retroid Pocket Duo (Mr. Purple T30): menus, Road to the Show (including create-a-player and a loaded career), exhibitions with real teams.** Frame rates by device: the repository README's [Tested devices](../../../README.md#tested-devices) and [Performance](#performance) below. Skin, faces, hair colours and beards of generated players render since 1.0.24 (see [the skin and hair write-up](../../../docs/mlb-the-show-26-skin-and-hair.md)). The pack is made on a PC (Linux under WSL) from a `.nup` of the merged base and update; the apps come from `.github/workflows/test-apk.yml` (test) and `release-apk.yml` (official). The aim is the offline modes (Road to the Show, exhibition); the online modes need Sony's servers and are out of scope. The game imports `nn::socket`, `nn::ec`, `nn::friends`, `nn::account` and `nn::nifm` for them.

## Files and shaders

- RomFS: 86,057 files, 35.20 GB. Platform folders `NSW/`, `CROSS/`, `AND/`, `PS4/`, `FLUX/` at the top. Most files are `.tex` (38,014) and `.mod` (25,940).
- **Shaders: GLSLC outputs in `.fxbnvn` files.** `NSW/SHADERS` holds 456 `.fxbnvn` files (161.7 MB, 56 bytes to 2.1 MB). Each starts with `FXB\0`, then its own length and a header length; inside are whole GLSLC outputs (magic `0x19866891`), each with one control section (`0x98761234`, 0x880 bytes) and its code area right after it. 7,720 outputs over all files (7,234 graphics, 486 compute), nothing compressed and no BNSH. 3-file banks of 56 bytes hold no shader. The survey's note ("no BNSH / BAHS at the top level … must be located first") does not count GLSLC outputs; `shader_split.py` reads them through its loose-file GLSLC path (`scan_whole` → `glslc_shaders`) with no new reader. `dm/tools/fxbnvn_probe.py` prints this structure from a `.nup` without writing game data.
- `shader_split.py` found 3,445 shaders: 244 compute, 1,757 pixel, 1,444 vertex, all from `nsw` (the 7,720 outputs repeat across banks). 5,776 variation pairs, 244 compute answer rows, no `[RED]` line. Single-stage programs: the 1,444 vertex shaders are paired with a cover of 4 pixel shaders.

- Modules: 0x5a00000 (90 MB) after the rewrite. With the default 64 MB of used memory, `main` asked for a 0xc9400000 heap (pool − 64 MB) and `SetHeapSize` refused it. With `mem_used 0x6000000` it asks for 0xc7500000 (3.11 GB). Not measured in play yet.

## Performance

Measured from the test app's logs (the `pace`, `[nvn] timing` and `longframe` lines; `DM_VK_GPUPROF=300` in the test app for this game), on the field in an exhibition, 1.0.19.

- **AYN Thor (Snapdragon 8 Gen 2), current.** About 34 fps (0.2.80, fast cores off and on alike). A frame is about 31 ms: the game's main thread runs 27–28 ms of CPU in it (guest waits 7–8 ms of that), the replay thread records in 12–13 ms, the GPU is busy about 18 ms, NVN submit and present about 0.1 ms. CPU-bound on the main thread; not thermal (headroom not reported, clocks at their caps).
- **Sony Xperia 1 V (Snapdragon 8 Gen 2), current.** About 18 fps with fast cores off, 20.6 on (0.2.80). Main thread about 50 ms of CPU a frame, GPU 36–45 ms, replay about 24 ms; thermal headroom 0 and CPU clocks capped (2.59 and 2.84 GHz), so it throttles. Scene pass 1440×810. Costliest GPU work: `PLAYERCOMPUTESHADERS` e0818838838d85df (14 dispatches, about 6.2 ms, a per-vertex collision loop of up to 500 iterations), `SKINNINGCOMPUTE` 7f78de8d31ad556c (about 6.3 ms), 212 draws of d214bad0d2a436d7 (5.4 ms).
- **The fastest core to the main thread instead of the replay thread (tried, reverted, 0.2.81).** Fast cores gave the busiest thread the 3.19 GHz core; that was the game's main thread. Its CPU time a frame did not drop (28.3 ms against 27.3 ms on a 2.8 GHz core) and the field fell to about 31 fps, so the replay thread keeps the fastest core (0.2.82). The main thread's time does not follow the core's clock: what it spends it on is open.
- **Retroid Pocket Duo (Adreno 722), current.** Drivers: Mr. Purple T30 (PurpleVK 26.3.0) draws best (player-reported glitches fewest); T23 and T26 make no Vulkan device; Turnip Gen8 V39 and Turnip Duo v5.1 showed more flicker (menus, skin black and back; tried before the fixes of 0.2.105 and 0.2.121). T30, 0.2.84: about 24 fps on the field, GPU p50 16.5 ms (24.5 on Turnip Gen8 V39), replay 17 ms, frame work about 38 ms; thermal status 3. Errors only the crowd shader skip and the texture ID past the table (as on the Thor); the flicker leaves nothing in the log.
- **Main thread inside NVN calls (0.2.84, RP Duo, T30).** About 12–13 ms of the 38 ms frame (`DM_NVN_PROF`). The largest are waits for the GPU: `SyncWait` about 4–5 ms and `EventGetValue` (the game polling an event) about 4 ms a frame; then trivial getters (`CommandBufferIsRecording`, `MemoryPoolGetBufferAddress`, `DeviceGetSeparateTextureHandle`) at about 2 ms together, much of it the timing wrapper's own cost. Submit 0.2 ms.
- **Crowd control (0.2.86).** The profile's `crowd` row lists the 19 `CROWDSHADER` vertex shaders; Game settings › Crowd control hides their draws (`DM_VK_SKIP_VS`). They are about 15 % of the field's GPU time (5.4 of 37 ms on the Xperia, 2.5 of 18 on the Thor, 2.4 of 15.5 on the RP Duo with T30) and 320–410 draws a frame. The game's own crowd work on the main thread stays. Measured (Thor, Turnip Gen8 V39, 0.2.90 against 0.2.84, exhibition on the field): 33.6 → about 52 fps; GPU busy 19.5 → 14.0 ms; replay recording 12.0 → 6.8 ms; main thread frame work 27.2 → 19.0 ms, of it inside NVN calls 10.9 → 6.7 ms, `SyncWait` and `EventGetValue` polling out of the top five. Far more than the crowd's GPU share: its draws sat in the replay-to-GPU chain the main thread waits on (below).
- **The main thread waits on the previous frame (current finding, 0.2.84 logs).** On the Thor the time from the replay's start to the GPU's end is 30–32 ms, about the whole frame (30 fps), while the replay records 13–17 ms and the GPU is busy 18–20 ms. On the RP Duo about 8–9 ms of the main thread's frame is `SyncWait` and polling `EventGetValue`. GPU events and syncs land only when the whole submission's fence completes (`vka_exec.c` completion effects), so the game's wait lasts until replay and GPU are both done. Polling counts as CPU time, which would explain why the faster core did not shorten the main thread. 0.2.90's `DM_VK_SYNCLOG=400` logged nothing: the game's waits do not block in `mtl_wait` (it polls). With the crowd hidden, the Thor's replay start to GPU end is 17–19 ms against a 6.8 ms recording and 14 ms GPU, so the GPU barely runs while the frame is recorded (parts every 600 draws: about 3 queue submits a frame). 0.2.92 tries `DM_VK_CHUNK=150` and `DM_VK_CHUNK_PASS=300` in the test app for this game, so the GPU starts sooner, and drops `DM_NVN_PROF` (its wrapper costs main-thread time on every NVN call). Result (Thor, crowd hidden): 51.8 → 54.0 fps, replay start to GPU end about 18.5 → 17.2 ms, 5 queue submits a frame; part of the gain is `DM_NVN_PROF` gone. 0.2.94 makes 150 / 300 the host's defaults for every game. What stays: the main thread at about 16 ms of CPU in an 18.5 ms frame, now mostly the game's own code.
- **Peanuts Native 0.1.8 dev merged (0.2.96).** Brings upstream's engine work since this fork's base (2026-11-18): the KGSL clock floor that reaches the clock on 8 Gen 2 (upstream measured the Thor's GPU at 401–550 of 680 MHz without it), shader parts and compute pipelines made before the game starts, 150-draw submission parts with 150 inside a pass, audio that follows the game's pace, texture upload fixes. Not measured on this game yet.
- **Xperia after the 0.1.8 dev merge (0.2.98, crowd hidden, Turnip Gen8 V39).** Handheld (720p) 56.7 fps (44.3 before the merge), docked 49.5 (33.3 before); the GPU clock floor beat is on. Thermal headroom ran out late in the session (CPU clocks capped at 1.65 and 1.98 GHz). After an audio focus interruption at about 157 s every output was lost again every 2 s for the rest of the session (244 stream reopens, the silent sink in between; `hle_audio.c` watchdog). Open: 0.2.101 logs the AAudio error code.
- **Xperia, 0.2.104, docked 1080p, crowd hidden, 19 minutes.** 51.6 fps on average: 60 at the start, 43 at the end, as the phone throttled the fast cores from 2.84 to 1.25 GHz (headroom 0); GPU about 13.5 ms. Heat is this phone's limit now. No audio interruption in this run.
- **Texture ID 65536 beyond the table (every device, current).** The game registers an image at pool ID 65536; the storage-image table held 65536 (IDs 0–65535), so its descriptor was never written and a compute shader's writes to it went nowhere. 0.2.105 sizes the image table at 1 << 17: gone from the Retroid Pocket Duo's 0.2.109 log (resolved).
- **Retroid Pocket Duo, 0.2.109, Mr. Purple T30, crowd hidden, 17 minutes.** Docked 33–35 fps (GPU 14–15 ms, replay 10–12, replay start to GPU end 26–28 ms), handheld 43.5 fps (GPU 10.6 ms). CPU clocks stayed at their tops (2.80 / 2.40 / 1.84 GHz); thermal status 5 with headroom 1.5. The main thread is the limit on this CPU.
- **Skin flickering black and back on the Retroid Pocket Duo (fixed in 0.2.121: the driver's tiled rendering).** Only there (Adreno 722; every driver tried, least with Mr. Purple T30); the menu flicker went with the image table fix (0.2.105). The log shows nothing at the moments it happens (no evictions: the image cache held 44 MiB of a 2 GiB limit). First test, 0.2.111–0.2.113 (test app, that device only): every game barrier full (`DM_VK_BAR_NARROW=0`) and a barrier after every dispatch (`DM_VK_DISPATCH_ORDER=1`): the flicker stayed, and blocks of noise showed over the uniform screen's player and background (video, 2026-11-25). Second test, 0.2.114–0.2.115: no UBWC compression on images (`DM_VK_UBWC=0`): the flicker stayed, maybe worse; frame rate unchanged (37.7 fps in game, as on 0.2.109). Third test, 0.2.116: the driver's compiler without 16-bit math (`IR3_SHADER_DEBUG=nofp16`), shader caches off for the test (`DM_VK_PCACHE=0`, `MESA_SHADER_CACHE_DISABLE=true`): the flicker stayed. Fourth test, 0.2.117: no tiled (GMEM) rendering and no LRZ early depth (`TU_DEBUG=sysmem,nolrz`), and no uniform-buffer promotion (`IR3_SHADER_DEBUG=nouboopt`), together: the skin flicker went (several innings, 2026-11-26), the noise blocks stayed; 43.8 fps in game. Fifth test, 0.2.118: `IR3_SHADER_DEBUG=nouboopt` alone: the flicker came back. Sixth test, 0.2.119: `TU_DEBUG=nolrz` alone (no LRZ early depth), shader caches back on: the flicker came back. Seventh test, 0.2.120: `TU_DEBUG=sysmem` alone (no tiled rendering): no flicker, 44.7 fps in game (the best of the test runs, GPU busy 10.7 ms a frame at the median). So the cause is Turnip's tiled (GMEM) rendering on the 722. Since 0.2.121 the app sets `TU_DEBUG=sysmem` for this game on the Retroid Pocket Duo (GameActivity); other drivers ignore it. Still open there: the blocks of noise over the uniform screen's player and background, which stayed with every setting tried. Screenshots (2026-11-26) put them only on pictures the game streams in during play: the stadium scoreboard's player photo, the batter's headshot (lower left) and the uniform screen's player; the field, HUD and logos are clean. Suspected: a streamed texture uploaded before the game finished filling its storage. 0.2.123 (test app, that device): `DM_VK_TEXLOG=1` logs every texture upload of 64x64 or more (vka_tex.c texlog) to find which ones and how they are filled. The movie planes (Bink, linear R8, re-uploaded as the game decodes) were the first suspect; 0.2.125 held their uploads until a quiet scan (`DM_VK_TEX_SETTLE`): the noise stayed and the movies got choppier, so it was taken out (0.2.127). Duo log 060617 points elsewhere: the noisy pictures are streamed block-compressed images (BC7 sRGB 1920x1080 for the uniform screen's player, 400x400 and 512x512 BC7 portraits). A 1920x1080 one was first uploaded with 30 % of its storage still zero, then one change was seen near its end, and no more. 0.2.127 (test app, the Duo): `DM_VK_TEX_AUDIT=2` hashes tracked textures' storage in the background, logs any change the dirty-page tracking did not see (`audit-miss`) and uploads it. Duo log 062501: no change was missed, and the noise stayed; the uploads match the storage. (The 1920x1080 BC7's 30 % of zeros is its layout's padding: 270 block rows laid out as 384.) 0.2.128 (test app, every device): the hashes of each large block-compressed picture's storage by 64 KiB (`texlog chunks`), to compare the same screen on the Duo and the Thor: the same bytes mean the Duo's GPU or driver shows them wrong; different bytes mean they are wrong in memory already. Result (Thor and Duo logs 064128, Dodgers in both): every picture loaded from the files that both runs loaded has the same bytes on both devices, the 1920x1080 BC7 ones too. The noisy pictures are the ones the game makes as it plays: it renders a player and encodes the picture to BC7 / BC1 on the GPU, writing the blocks as RGBA32UI / RG32UI texels over the compressed texture's storage (`storage take` and `bc alias sync` in the log: 512x512 and 768x768 BC7, 512x256 and 1024x512 BC1). The replay moves those texels into the compressed image with vkCmdCopyImage. 0.2.129 (test app, the Duo): that copy goes through a buffer instead (`DM_VK_BC_VIA_BUFFER=1`, vka_tex.c `bc_copy`), as uploads from files do. Duo log 070234: the copy went through the buffer (768x768 BC7, 512x256 BC1), and the noise stayed: the encoded blocks are wrong before the copy. Taken out (0.2.130). Subgroups differ between the two: the Duo's driver (Mr. Purple T30) lets every stage pin 64-lane subgroups, so its vertex and tessellation stages run pinned at 64; the Thor's Turnip pins only fragment and compute, and the rest run one-lane subgroups. 0.2.130 (test app, the Duo): only fragment pinned, compute and the rest one-lane (`DM_VK_SUBGROUP_PIN=0x10`). Duo log 083446: applied, the noise stayed (46.5 fps in game). 0.2.131 (test app, the Duo): the driver's compiler with a wait on every instruction and no preambles (`IR3_SHADER_DEBUG=fullsync,nopreamble`), shader caches off. Result (2026-11-26): fewer noise blocks, and on the portraits and the scoreboard only about half the time: a race, made rarer by slower, stricter execution. 0.2.134 (test app, the Duo): compute shuffles with the instruction, not through workgroup memory (`DM_VK_SHUFFLE_LDS=0`); the workgroup-memory modules take a subgroup's lanes as running in step. Not played yet when the next pattern came up: the owner recalls the noise only about half the time in 0.2.117 too. The tests that made it rarer (0.2.117, 0.2.118, 0.2.131) all had the shader caches off; with them on (0.2.119-0.2.130) it was there every time. 0.2.135 (test app, the Duo): the caches off alone. Result: noise every time on the portraits and the scoreboard, so not the caches. What 0.2.117, 0.2.118 and 0.2.131 share is how constants reach a shader: no uniform-buffer promotion, or no preambles (where the promoted loads run). 0.2.136 (test app, the Duo): `IR3_SHADER_DEBUG=nouboopt,nopreamble,nodescprefetch`, caches off. Also: logs now go up as `<device model>-<name>.gz`; two devices' logs of the same second had one name, and the second was dropped as already sent.
- **Open: what the main thread spends its time on.** The test app sets `DM_NVN_PROF=1` for this game (0.2.82; it took effect only from 0.2.84, once the host armed it at the first `nvnBootstrapLoader` instead of in a constructor that runs before the launcher sets the environment), so the log splits the NVN calls' share (the `[nvn] translation layer top 5` line) from the game's own code.

Glitch fixes: the image table (0.2.105, menu flicker and texture ID 65536), no tiled rendering on the Retroid Pocket Duo (0.2.121, skin flicker). ## Found on this game

Boot and first frames:

- **Heap sized from the application pool less used memory (resolved).** First boot on an AYN Thor: `[mem] SetHeapSize 0xc9400000 + modules 0x5a00000 > app pool 0xcd500000`, then `nn::diag abort: Result 0x1003` at `main+0x5b1b60` in `nninitStartup`. Fixed by `mem_used 0x6000000`. [Heap sized from the application pool less used memory](../../../docs/reference/memory.md#heap-sized-from-the-application-pool-less-used-memory)
- **Opening video: nsd (resolved).** At `ATT_SCREEN: FMV player init` the game aborted (`Result 0xe8d`, `sdk+0x3f4cfc`) in `nn::nsd::GetApplicationServerEnvironmentType`, reached through `nn::oe::GetServerEnvironmentType`. The host answers that import with retail 0 (`hle_offline.c`).
- **Voice chat AudioOut used before it is opened (resolved).** COnline asks the headset output's channel count (`main+0x18cd30c`, `main+0x18cd674`) before the headset thread opens it; the host answers as for a closed output (`hle_audio.c`, `ao_or_closed`).
- **ContainsAudioOutBuffer (resolved).** A null read at `sdk+0x1e7e24` right after `audio #2 Start`: the host takes over `nn::audio::ContainsAudioOutBuffer`.
- **3D scene black: 119 textures in one bind (resolved).** The host refused binds of more than 32 texture handles whole; they are recorded in parts of 32 (`hle_nvn.c`, `bind_handles`).
- **Spikes, missing skin (resolved).** `SKINNINGCOMPUTE` and `PLAYERCOMPUTESHADERS` write storage buffer textures at offsets off the device's texel buffer alignment (64); such a binding is copied into aligned scratch memory before the dispatch and back after it (`vka_draw.c` tbuf_view, tbuf_writeback).

Generated players (create-a-player, Road to the Show). The whole investigation, with its wrong turns and the tools that found each cause, is in [docs/mlb-the-show-26-skin-and-hair.md](../../../docs/mlb-the-show-26-skin-and-hair.md):

- **The create-a-player model untextured (resolved, 0.2.20).** Its GPU-compressed textures (BC1/BC3/BC4/BC7, encoded by `TEXTURECOMPRESSBCN` compute through RG32UI and RGBA32UI views of the same storage) were all-zero blocks until the storage take covered compressed targets.
- **Generated faces black (resolved, 0.2.29).** The face's target skin tone comes from the CPU's read of the GPU-drawn skin tone palette (`CAP_SkinTonePalette`, 256x512 RGBA8; main+0x1688ae4 through `nvnTextureReadTexels`), which read the never-written storage. [`ReadTexels` of a texture the GPU wrote through another texture object](../../../docs/reference/nvn-objects.md#readtexels-of-a-texture-the-gpu-wrote-through-another-texture-object-vulkan-replay)
- **Hair tips and beards without colour (resolved, 0.2.30).** `HAIRCOMPUTE` 8cbd9b4be32e966d samples the BC1 hair albedo whose blocks were written through an RG32UI view; the storage take ran for draws only. [A compute dispatch samples a texture the GPU wrote through another format](../../../docs/reference/nvn-objects.md#a-compute-dispatch-samples-a-texture-the-gpu-wrote-through-another-format-vulkan-replay)
- **Body skin black on a loaded career (resolved, 1.0.24).** Compute 67d5da22e65373bf averages the skin tone palette into each body material's target tone (`ICE_SKINSHADER` fp_c4[2]); the palette's image had gone two frames after its texture object was finalized, and the later texture object over its storage got an empty image. [A GPU-drawn image goes before a later texture object over its storage reads it](../../../docs/reference/nvn-objects.md#a-gpu-drawn-image-goes-before-a-later-texture-object-over-its-storage-reads-it-vulkan-replay)
- **Frontend lighting (not a fault).** The menus' irradiance cube and light rig probe volumes are empty (0 lights: the bank points at the placeholder `nowhere` set); in a stadium the rigs hold hundreds of lights. The game's own state, not a host fault.

## Open

- **Hair in the player editor.** Some styles do not look right in the editor's close-up (reported after 1.0.24, not investigated yet). Hair colours were right in 0.2.30.
- **Real players black once.** In one session (an exhibition, Dodgers against Blue Jays, 1.0.22) every player's skin was black; the next exhibition was right. Not reproduced.
- **Background pipelines** are off by default for this game (`Prefs.psoAsync`, its own key `vk_pso_async2`): set off while chasing the black skin, which had another cause; the game has been played this way since. Whether it can be on again is not tested.
- **Noise blocks on the Retroid Pocket Duo.** Small blocks of random colour over pictures the game streams in during play (the stadium scoreboard's player photo, the batter's headshot, the uniform screen's player); not on the Thor or the Xperia. Being traced: see Performance, the Duo's skin flicker entry.
- **Sound off after a phone call.** Once, after a call came in during a game and was dismissed, the sound stayed off. The test app logs the audio stream's errors (`[RED] audio #N: AAudio error callback`) to catch it next time.
- **The game's main thread** is the frame rate's limit now on every device tested (see Performance).
- A crowd shader skipped (more than 512 descriptors): not looked at yet; Crowd control hides the crowd anyway. (The texture ID past the image table, 65536 and up, is fixed since 0.2.105.)
- SDK 19: NVN object sizes and service layouts differ by SDK; other SDK 19 profiles exist ([OCTOPATH TRAVELER 0](../01005270232F2000/README.md)).
